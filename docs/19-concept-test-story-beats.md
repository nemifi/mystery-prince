# Concept-Test Story Beats

Status: WORKING

## Purpose

Define the minimum polished dramatic/mystery beats needed to test H1 (star-system identity across ROLEs) and H2 (character emotion strengthens mystery reasoning).

These are not final 30-minute scripts and do not prescribe final UI. Each slice should be approximately 10–15 minutes when realized.

The same three PRINCES appear in both works:

- REI (`prince_alpha`)
- MINATO (`prince_beta`)
- KAI (`prince_gamma`)

---

# Slice A — THE 23:30 MESSAGE

## Core emotional test

The player should begin by trusting MINATO, become suspicious of REI, enjoy KAI's ambiguity, then experience resistance to the conclusion that MINATO is the culprit.

The desired final feeling is not merely "I solved it." It is:

> **"I didn't want the evidence to point at MINATO, but it did."**

## Beat 0 — Cold open: the impossible message

A man is found dead on a restricted luxury-hotel floor. His body condition indicates he could not have been alive at 23:30.

At exactly 23:30, everyone receives a voice message from the victim.

The voice is unmistakably his.

Immediate player question: **How can a dead man send a message?**

## Beat 1 — REI takes control

REI, hotel security director, enters and immediately reduces chaos into actionable facts. He does not demand authority; everyone simply begins responding to his questions.

Character attraction beat:

- He notices one apparently trivial inconsistency before anyone else.
- He gives the player a concrete task rather than reassurance.
- He remains calm while others argue.

Mystery suspicion beat:

REI controls the access logs and communication records. He therefore has the greatest apparent ability to manufacture the impossible message.

Desired reaction: **"He's impressive, but he's also the obvious person who could fake this."**

## Beat 2 — MINATO becomes the safe person

MINATO, the victim's private physician, checks the body and explains the medical situation without talking down to the player.

Character attraction beat:

- He notices the player's stress before discussing his own.
- He remembers a small detail from a conversation earlier in the slice.
- He gently reframes one frightening fact into something the player can act on.

He states that he saw the victim alive shortly before 23:30.

This creates the initial apparent solution: the medical estimate must be wrong, or the impossible message is not impossible.

Desired reaction: **"At least MINATO seems trustworthy."**

## Beat 3 — KAI turns suspicion into play

KAI, an invited illusionist, admits almost immediately that he had access to a restricted area he should not have entered.

He appears more entertained than frightened by being suspected.

Character attraction beat:

- He gives one technically true but intentionally misleading answer.
- When pressed, he points out a contradiction in someone else's wording rather than defending himself directly.

His unauthorized credential is real, but it does not explain the death.

Desired reaction: **"He is absolutely doing something wrong; I just don't know if it's murder."**

## Beat 4 — False solution: REI altered a record

The player discovers that one security record was altered.

REI initially refuses to explain why.

This should feel like the clean solution: REI had access, control, motive to hide something, and altered data.

When challenged, REI admits the alteration. He was covering an unauthorized search unrelated to the victim's death.

Important character beat:

REI does not ask the player to trust him. He gives enough verifiable information to allow the player to decide.

Desired reaction: **"He lied, but the way he handles being exposed still feels like REI."**

## Beat 5 — Reframe the impossible message

The hotel communication system supports scheduled delivery.

The 23:30 message proves only that the victim recorded it earlier, not that he was alive at 23:30.

This is the central reasoning turn of the slice.

The player must actively make or confirm this inference; it should not be delivered as exposition alone.

The apparent time boundary collapses.

## Beat 6 — MINATO's warmth becomes dangerous

Once the scheduled-message possibility is established, MINATO's claimed late sighting becomes the only evidence keeping the victim alive near 23:30.

A detail MINATO previously volunteered about the victim's room state can only be known by someone present during the earlier true death window, not during his claimed later visit.

The exact physical detail may change during script development, but it must satisfy three rules:

1. it was fairly presented before the accusation,
2. it cannot be explained by MINATO's professional knowledge alone,
3. it proves his timeline account is fabricated rather than merely mistaken.

MINATO remains emotionally attentive while the evidence turns against him.

Desired reaction: **"Please let there be another explanation."**

## Beat 7 — Player accusation

The player must explicitly choose MINATO and state/assemble the essential reasoning:

- the 23:30 message was scheduled,
- therefore the victim could already be dead,
- MINATO's claimed later sighting is false,
- MINATO possesses privileged knowledge tied to the real death window.

The accusation must be a player act, not an automatic cutscene.

## Beat 8 — Resolution: warmth was real

MINATO does not reveal that his kindness was fake. His care for people is precisely what led him into the crime: he chose a person over law/rule/truth and crossed an irreversible line.

The story must not excuse the crime.

The emotional point is that the same core trait that made MINATO attractive also made this ROLE capable of the offense.

Final H2 test signal:

> The player should feel that understanding MINATO helped solve the mystery, and solving the mystery deepened MINATO rather than destroying the character.

# Slice B — THE SEALED EXPRESS

## Core emotional test

The second slice should immediately communicate that this is a new world and new biographies, while REI/MINATO/KAI remain recognizable.

The desired meta reaction is:

> **"Oh, this time they're these people."**

—not confusion about continuity.

## Beat 0 — Cold open: the impossible theft

On an interwar luxury night train, a famous jewel is revealed to be a replica after a tunnel blackout.

The sealed display was never opened during the journey.

Immediate player question: **How was the jewel replaced?**

## Beat 1 — KAI is the authority now

KAI is the chief conductor.

This deliberately breaks the expectation that his core requires outsider/criminal status.

Character identity beat:

- He knows the rules better than anyone precisely because he enjoys finding where they bend.
- He uses humor while controlling a tense carriage.
- He gives the player a technically correct answer that conceals one procedural violation.

Desired H1 reaction: **"Even as the conductor, that's obviously KAI."**

## Beat 2 — REI protects the object

REI is the exhibition curator/diplomatic aide responsible for the jewel.

He is precise, composed, and furious with himself more than anyone else.

Character attraction beat:

He turns a chaotic accusation into a finite set of possible access windows.

Because his ROLE is built around protecting the jewel, he initially feels like the person least likely to have stolen it.

## Beat 3 — MINATO is the investigator

MINATO is the railway investigator.

Unlike a purely procedural investigator, he pays attention to how witnesses describe what they felt and noticed.

He recovers a small human observation dismissed as irrelevant by the formal report.

Desired H1 reaction after Slice A: **"I know what happened last time, but this MINATO really does feel safe."**

The player should notice the tension between meta-memory and current-work evidence.

## Beat 4 — False solution: KAI broke procedure

KAI is exposed as having violated train procedure during the relevant journey.

This provides a plausible theft route and temporarily makes him the cleanest suspect.

When challenged, his violation turns out to serve an unrelated purpose.

Important test: the explanation should not sanitize KAI. He still chose autonomy over rules; he simply did not steal the jewel.

## Beat 5 — Reframe the crime window

A pre-departure image is inspected closely.

A distinctive feature proves the replica was already in the display before the train left.

Therefore the tunnel blackout is not the crime window at all.

This must be the central reasoning action of the slice.

The mystery changes from:

> "Who opened the display during the blackout?"

into:

> **"Who could replace the jewel before the display was sealed?"**

## Beat 6 — REI's competence becomes evidence

REI had pre-departure custody access.

That alone is not enough.

The player then notices REI describing a subtle feature of the authenticated original with a precision that cannot come from the replica currently on display or from the public catalog.

The same observational precision that made REI attractive in Slice A now becomes evidence of privileged contact with the original.

Desired reaction: **"That is such a REI way to get caught."**

## Beat 7 — Player accusation

The player must explicitly accuse REI and establish:

- the replica predates departure,
- therefore the blackout is deliberate misdirection,
- REI had the necessary pre-sealing access,
- REI possesses knowledge consistent with direct handling of the original.

## Beat 8 — Resolution: identity without biography

REI accepts the logical loss before giving an emotional explanation.

The explanation belongs entirely to this EXPERIENCE; it must not reference the hotel story or imply a shared biography.

The audience should nevertheless recognize the same decision style, emotional restraint, and response to being cornered.

Final H1 test signal:

> **"REI was innocent-but-secretive in A and guilty in B, but both felt like REI."**

# Cross-slice test requirements

## Do not rely on continuity exposition

Never explain the star system inside the fictional story. The app/brand layer may present the casting, but the characters should behave as native to the current EXPERIENCE.

## Avoid repeated narrative morality

A PRINCE must not become predictable as "always innocent," "always the killer," or "always the ally."

Across these two slices:

- REI: false suspect -> culprit
- MINATO: culprit -> genuine ally
- KAI: suspicious outsider -> suspicious authority

A future third test should preferably make KAI the reliable ally or emotional center to stress his core further.

## Measurement priority

After both slices, ask behaviorally oriented questions before explaining the concept:

1. Which person did you most want to keep watching in A? In B?
2. Did anyone feel like a different person despite having the same identity?
3. Did the different ROLE make any PRINCE more interesting?
4. At any point did liking a character make it harder to accept the evidence?
5. If the same three appeared in a third unrelated mystery, would you want to see what roles they receive?

The strongest spontaneous phrase remains: **"今回の○○" / "this version of X."**

# Prototype PRINCE Visual Identity

Status: WORKING

## Goal

Define visual identity for the concept-test PRINCES in a way that survives radical ROLE, costume, era, and world changes.

The visual system must support the star-system hypothesis:

> A PRINCE should still read as the same star even when the fictional biography changes completely.

Therefore the design should not depend on one occupation, uniform, accessory, era, faction, or story-world symbol.

## Two-layer model

### VISUAL CORE — relatively stable

- face geometry and proportions
- eye/brow shape and gaze behavior
- mouth/resting-expression character
- hair silhouette family
- body proportion / build
- posture and center of gravity
- characteristic micro-expression
- characteristic gesture language
- broad voice/physical-presence impression

### ROLE SHELL — freely variable

- clothing
- hairstyle styling within the core silhouette family
- accessories
- profession-specific props
- historical/futuristic styling
- social status presentation
- apparent authority
- cleanliness / damage / fatigue
- role-specific palette
- moral framing

Rule: ROLE SHELL may strongly transform the first impression, but should not replace VISUAL CORE.

## Prototype portfolio

### REI — control / intelligence / authority

Core impression: the person people look toward when uncertainty rises.

Visual Core:
- tall, lean, controlled silhouette; vertical lines rather than broad mass
- narrow-to-medium eyes with a steady, measuring gaze
- straight, clean brow line
- restrained mouth; smiles are rare enough to matter
- dark hair with a deliberate, clean silhouette; styling may change by ROLE but should avoid chaotic volume
- posture is centered and economical; almost no wasted movement
- tends to face situations directly instead of angling away
- signature micro-behavior: a brief pause before irreversible decisions, often paired with a tiny ordering/straightening gesture

Role A expression — Hotel security director:
- modern, severe, operational competence
- sharp tailoring / controlled grooming
- visually reads as the person who already has a plan

Role B expression — Exhibition curator / diplomatic aide:
- refined rather than tactical
- period tailoring, gloves or formal presentation as ROLE assets
- same measured gaze and precise body language
- must still read as REI even if the outfit and social register are completely different

Do not make the permanent identity depend on: suit, tie, police imagery, glasses, weapon, uniform, or one fixed hairstyle.

### MINATO — warmth / trust / human attention

Core impression: the person who makes the user feel seen before trust has actually been earned.

Visual Core:
- approachable, balanced silhouette; slightly softer body language than REI
- warm, attentive eyes; gaze tends to stay on the other person rather than scan the room
- brows and mouth capable of subtle sympathetic reactions
- hair should carry soft movement rather than severe geometry
- posture reduces distance: slight lean, angled shoulders, attentive stillness
- expressions transition smoothly instead of snapping between extremes
- signature micro-behavior: small head inclination / quiet eye contact before answering something personal

Role A expression — Private physician:
- reassuring cleanliness and emotional availability
- visual trustworthiness should make later suspicion emotionally costly

Role B expression — Railway investigator:
- more active, practical, observant presentation
- coat, period fieldwear, notebook/official props belong to ROLE only
- despite the harder investigative ROLE, the user should still recognize the same human-centered attention

Do not make the permanent identity depend on: doctor coat, healer coding, romance coding, soft colors, one profession, or permanent innocence.

### KAI — danger / freedom / play

Core impression: the person whose intentions cannot be completely fixed, making him difficult to ignore.

Visual Core:
- lean, flexible silhouette with visible asymmetry in stance
- sharper, more playful eye shape; gaze often feels like it has noticed the game behind the conversation
- expressive mouth / half-smile potential
- hair silhouette allows controlled disorder or asymmetry
- posture often off-axis: leaning, turning, occupying space less formally than the others
- movement can be quick or lazy, but rarely institutional
- signature micro-behavior: looks amused at the exact moment other people become certain

Role A expression — Illusionist:
- theatrical ROLE shell may amplify asymmetry and danger
- should not become a permanent magician archetype

Role B expression — Chief conductor:
- intentionally tests the system: disciplined uniform and procedural authority placed on the least institutionally obedient PRINCE
- uniform should contrast with rather than erase KAI's off-center posture and playful gaze

Do not make the permanent identity depend on: criminal styling, earrings, cards, magic motifs, black clothing, flirtation, or villain coding.

## Separation test

A successful character sheet should pass these tests:

1. **Silhouette test** — simplified body/posture silhouettes should remain distinguishable.
2. **Grayscale face test** — without costume color or props, the three faces should remain clearly different.
3. **Cross-ROLE test** — REI-A vs REI-B should feel more identical than REI-A vs MINATO-A despite radically different clothes.
4. **Expression test** — the same emotional event should produce character-specific expressions rather than generic anime expression swaps.
5. **No-prop test** — removing glasses, jewelry, weapons, profession props, hats, and uniforms must not destroy identity.

## Initial concept-test asset set

Per PRINCE:

- neutral bust / identity reference
- full-body neutral posture reference
- EXPERIENCE A ROLE design
- EXPERIENCE B ROLE design
- five expression anchors:
  - neutral / resting
  - attraction / warmth or interest
  - suspicion / guardedness
  - emotional fracture / loss of composure
  - post-reveal / decisive expression
- two characteristic gesture references

For H1 validation, A/B ROLE sheets should be shown together only after the tester has experienced both works. During play, the system should not explicitly explain that the ROLEs are alternate versions; recognition should emerge naturally.

## Design objective

The viewer should be able to say:

- “REI looks completely different in this world, but it is obviously REI.”
- “MINATO feels safe in both roles, but that safety has a different meaning.”
- “KAI looks unusually proper this time, yet the way he looks at people is still KAI.”

If recognition requires lore explanation or repeated costume motifs, the visual core is too weak.

# Prototype PRINCE Portfolio

Status: WORKING / TEST CAST

## Purpose

These three PRINCES are not intended to be the final launch cast. They are deliberately differentiated test identities used to validate whether a PRINCE can retain recognizability and appeal while ROLE, biography, world, morality, and narrative function change between EXPERIENCES.

The public-facing PRINCE concept remains broad. These are concrete instances, not category rules.

## Naming rule for the test

Use short star names rather than fixed full biographies. Working names:

- PRINCE ALPHA -> **REI / レイ**
- PRINCE BETA -> **MINATO / ミナト**
- PRINCE GAMMA -> **KAI / カイ**

The internal IDs remain `prince_alpha`, `prince_beta`, and `prince_gamma` during the prototype so the content fixtures do not need a meaningless identity migration.

## REI / レイ

### Audience promise

**The man people look at when nobody knows what to do next.**

He creates attraction through composure, competence, and the feeling that he sees one layer deeper than everyone else.

### Portable core

- **Appeal:** intellectual control and quiet authority
- **Decision principle:** reduce ambiguity first; choose actionable truth over comforting uncertainty
- **Interpersonal style:** sets the agenda without loudly claiming leadership; asks precise questions; protects through decisions more than reassurance
- **Emotional pattern:** normally controlled; strongest visible reaction when someone takes an unrequested risk or sacrifice for him
- **Signature:** short declarative phrasing, deliberate pauses before irreversible decisions, a small self-ordering gesture before a difficult answer

### Visual identity direction

Sharp rather than soft. Clean silhouette. Controlled posture. Eyes that feel observant rather than expressive. One recurring personal motif should survive ROLE changes, but clothing must belong entirely to the current EXPERIENCE.

Do not encode police, detective, executive, nobility, innocence, goodness, age, or fixed social rank into his permanent design.

### Test stress

REI must remain recognizable when his narrative position flips from **highly suspicious authority** to **actual culprit**.

### EXPERIENCE A — THE 23:30 MESSAGE

ROLE: hotel security director.

He controls access records and incident response. The player reasonably suspects him because he altered one record. His calm competence should make him both reassuring and intimidating. His secret is real but unrelated to the killing.

Desired reaction: **"He is obviously hiding something, but I still want him in charge."**

### EXPERIENCE B — THE SEALED EXPRESS

ROLE: exhibition curator / diplomatic aide.

He is responsible for protecting the jewel and is actually the culprit. The same precision and competence that made him attractive in A becomes incriminating knowledge in B.

Desired reaction: **"Of course REI would commit the crime this carefully."** Not because REI is always criminal, but because the ROLE weaponizes his existing core.

## MINATO / ミナト

### Audience promise

**The man who makes you feel seen before you have decided whether you should trust him.**

He creates attraction through warmth, attention, emotional intelligence, and natural closeness.

### Portable core

- **Appeal:** warmth and personal attention
- **Decision principle:** when abstract rules and a concrete person collide, instinctively prioritize the person
- **Interpersonal style:** closes distance naturally, remembers small details, gives people emotional room, uses names, makes trust feel easy
- **Emotional pattern:** usually gentle; anger becomes unexpectedly sharp when trust, vulnerability, or affection is exploited
- **Signature:** soft humor under tension, attentive silence, small personal observations, warmth that can become unsettling once doubt appears

### Visual identity direction

Approachable before glamorous. Open body language and an expression that makes eye contact feel personal. The design must still work when the ROLE is dangerous or morally compromised; avoid permanent healer/angel symbolism.

### Test stress

MINATO must remain attractive when his warmth changes from **the player's safest emotional space** into **the mechanism that makes the player lower their guard**, then later returns as genuine support in a different EXPERIENCE.

### EXPERIENCE A — THE 23:30 MESSAGE

ROLE: victim's private physician.

He is the player's warmest apparent ally and the actual culprit. His motive comes from protecting another person, but the mystery must not excuse the crime. His care should be real even while his account of the timeline is false.

Desired reaction: **"I don't want it to be him."**

### EXPERIENCE B — THE SEALED EXPRESS

ROLE: railway investigator.

He is genuinely on the player's side. His tendency to listen to people rather than only systems helps recover a testimony or detail others dismiss.

Desired reaction: **"This time I can trust MINATO... probably."** The memory of A should create meta-tension without becoming story continuity.

## KAI / カイ

### Audience promise

**The man you keep watching because you cannot tell whether he is helping, playing, or testing you.**

He creates attraction through autonomy, danger, humor, and controlled unpredictability.

### Portable core

- **Appeal:** danger, unpredictability, playful intelligence
- **Decision principle:** protect autonomy and curiosity before obedience
- **Interpersonal style:** tests boundaries, answers questions with better questions, reveals partial truths, provokes people into exposing themselves
- **Emotional pattern:** amusement covers most reactions; genuine loss of composure appears when trapped, possessed, or denied agency
- **Signature:** playful calm during danger, verbal reversals, technically honest half-truths, turning another person's certainty back on them

### Visual identity direction

A mobile, relaxed silhouette rather than rigid authority. A slight asymmetry or unconventional detail can survive ROLE changes, but do not permanently code him as criminal, supernatural, flirtatious, youthful, or villainous.

### Test stress

KAI must remain recognizable even when placed inside institutional authority rather than outsider status.

### EXPERIENCE A — THE 23:30 MESSAGE

ROLE: invited illusionist.

He has unauthorized access for an unrelated purpose and enjoys being suspected more than is socially reasonable. He is a red herring but should also provide one useful observation precisely because he notices how people react under pressure.

Desired reaction: **"He is definitely doing something wrong. I just don't know if it's this."**

### EXPERIENCE B — THE SEALED EXPRESS

ROLE: chief conductor.

He is now the person responsible for procedure and order, but still bends one rule for his own reason. The contrast tests whether KAI can occupy authority without becoming a different character.

Desired reaction: **"Even in uniform, KAI is still KAI."**

## Portfolio-level validation

The three test identities intentionally occupy different attraction vectors:

- **REI:** competence / authority / truth
- **MINATO:** intimacy / empathy / trust
- **KAI:** danger / autonomy / uncertainty

A successful test does not require equal popularity. It requires clear identity recognition and evidence that ROLE changes create additional appeal rather than identity collapse.

The strongest qualitative signals are:

- "This still feels like REI/MINATO/KAI."
- "I like this version of him."
- "I want to see what ROLE he gets next."
- "I knew his personality, but I couldn't assume whether he was innocent."

Failure signals include:

- "These are just different characters with the same face."
- "Why did his personality change?"
- "If roles reset every time, I don't care about him."
- "I already know what he will do because this character type is always innocent/guilty."

# MASTER WORK A — “THE 23:30 MESSAGE”

Status: WORKING / PROTOTYPE FIXTURE

Purpose: test PRINCE identity, emotional suspicion, and the Experience Contract with a contemporary closed-circle mystery.

This is not yet launch canon.

## One-line hook

A man is found dead in a luxury hotel's sealed private floor—yet at 23:30, after he should already be dead, everyone receives a message in his own voice saying he is alive and does not want to be disturbed.

## Intended player feeling

Trust should become evidence against itself. The person who feels safest to rely on should gradually become the person the player is most reluctant to question.

## World

Contemporary luxury hotel during a private high-profile event. A temporary lockdown after a building fault limits the relevant cast and locations.

## Prototype casting

### PRINCE ALPHA — Hotel security director
Publicly the most competent authority in the room. He controls access records and the response to the incident, making his composure useful and suspicious at the same time.

### PRINCE BETA — Victim's private physician
Warm, attentive, and immediately focused on keeping everyone calm. He becomes the player's most natural emotional ally.

### PRINCE GAMMA — Invited illusionist / performer
Known to enjoy testing rules and other people's certainty. He has an unrelated reason to possess access he should not have.

## Incident

The victim is found dead in a private lounge on a hotel floor with heavily restricted access. The preliminary sequence suggests death occurred before 23:30. At exactly 23:30, however, a message in the victim's own voice is delivered through the hotel's communication system: “I'm fine. Don't let anyone in until midnight.”

The apparent contradiction creates the central mystery.

## Player question

How could the victim send the 23:30 message if he was already dead—and which of the three men is using that contradiction to hide the true timeline?

## Full truth

The 23:30 message was not live. It had been recorded and scheduled earlier by the victim for an unrelated private purpose.

PRINCE BETA discovered that scheduled message before the incident reached the rest of the floor and chose to exploit it. He had already caused the victim's death earlier in the evening and then lied about the last time he saw the victim alive. The scheduled message created a false later boundary for the death window and made other people's movements appear more suspicious than his own.

His experience-specific motive is protective rather than acquisitive: the victim was using private information to destroy someone BETA had chosen to protect. BETA's PRINCE CORE remains intact—he prioritizes a specific person over an abstract rule—but the ROLE takes that principle to a morally unacceptable extreme.

ALPHA did alter one security record, but only to conceal an unauthorized search he conducted because he suspected the victim was hiding dangerous leverage on a guest. His lie creates the strongest mid-story false solution.

GAMMA possessed an illicit access credential and entered a restricted service area, but did so for an unrelated personal objective. His secret explains apparently incriminating access without making him the killer.

## Fair-play clue set

The prototype should expose enough information for the player to establish, before the final accusation:

1. The communication system supports scheduled delivery.
2. The 23:30 message was created substantially earlier than 23:30.
3. BETA's claimed last-seen time conflicts with at least one independent fact.
4. ALPHA's altered record explains a real secret but not the victim's death.
5. GAMMA's unauthorized access explains opportunity to mislead but not the false time boundary.
6. BETA knows a detail about the victim's condition or room state that he should not know under his stated timeline.

The final solution should rely on the combination of timeline and knowledge, not on motive alone.

## Dramatic spine

1. Impossible signal: the dead man apparently speaks at 23:30.
2. ALPHA appears to own the truth because he owns the records.
3. Evidence reveals ALPHA manipulated a record; he becomes the likely culprit.
4. The manipulation is exposed as a separate secret, collapsing the first solution.
5. GAMMA's illicit access creates a second plausible route, but it cannot explain the 23:30 contradiction.
6. The player realizes the central assumption is wrong: the message proves only delivery time, not recording time.
7. BETA's warm reliability becomes disturbing because his testimony contains knowledge available only to someone present earlier.
8. The player must accuse the person they were encouraged to trust.

## Character-value target

- ALPHA: competence can look like control, and control can look like guilt.
- BETA: warmth should make the accusation emotionally costly.
- GAMMA: suspicious behavior should be entertaining even when it is not the central crime.

## Aftertaste

The player should finish with two simultaneous reactions:

- “That was a fair reversal; I should have questioned the time assumption.”
- “I trusted BETA because of who he is, and the mystery used that against me.”

Do not add lore connecting this work to any other EXPERIENCE. Everything biography-specific belongs only to this CASTING.

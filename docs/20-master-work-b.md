# MASTER WORK B — “THE SEALED EXPRESS”

Status: WORKING / PROTOTYPE FIXTURE

Purpose: stress-test the star-system hypothesis by recasting the same three PRINCES into a different era, social structure, genre tone, and moral configuration.

This is not yet launch canon.

## One-line hook

On a 1920s-style international night train, a famous jewel is discovered to be a replica after a tunnel blackout—even though its locked display was never opened during the journey.

## Intended player feeling

The obvious “moment of the crime” should turn out to be theatrical misdirection. The player should have to abandon the exciting event and reason backward to a quieter truth.

## World

Stylized interwar luxury night train carrying diplomats, wealthy passengers, press, and a publicly exhibited jewel.

## Prototype casting

### PRINCE ALPHA — Exhibition curator and diplomatic aide
No police authority. He is elegant, exact, and apparently the person most committed to preserving order around the jewel. In this work, that same control and precision belong to the culprit.

### PRINCE BETA — Railway investigator
Officially responsible for passenger safety and the inquiry. He is unusually attentive to people rather than procedure and becomes the player's cooperative human anchor.

### PRINCE GAMMA — Chief conductor
He occupies a rule-bound institutional role but performs it in a distinctly GAMMA way: he tests statements, bends minor procedure, and seems amused by people's certainty. He is concealing a separate rule violation.

## Incident

The jewel travels in a locked transparent display. During a long tunnel passage, the train briefly loses light and an alarm sounds. When the lights return, the case is still locked and its seals appear intact, but experts soon identify the jewel inside as a near-perfect replica.

Everyone naturally treats the blackout as the theft window.

## Player question

How was the jewel replaced without opening the display during the journey—and why was the tunnel blackout made to look like the critical moment?

## Full truth

The jewel was already a replica when the train departed.

PRINCE ALPHA, the curator responsible for final exhibition preparation, replaced the real jewel before departure and then arranged for the tunnel alarm to create a memorable false crime window. His plan depended less on physically impossible theft than on controlling what everyone believed they had witnessed.

His experience-specific motive is ideological and personal: he believes the planned transfer of the jewel is itself illegitimate and intends to return the original through a private channel. The motive does not make the act lawful, but it prevents the ROLE from collapsing into a generic greedy thief.

BETA is genuinely investigating and becomes the player's ally. His tendency to prioritize people over procedure causes friction with official protocol but helps uncover testimony other investigators would dismiss.

GAMMA tampered with a minor train procedure for an unrelated reason: he allowed an unregistered passenger to travel in a service compartment. That secret makes his control over the tunnel period highly suspicious while ultimately proving irrelevant to the jewel substitution.

## Fair-play clue set

The prototype should expose enough information for the player to establish:

1. The display seals were continuously intact during the journey.
2. A pre-departure image already shows a subtle characteristic of the replica rather than the authenticated original.
3. The blackout alarm does not demonstrate access to the jewel; it only creates attention and a remembered time boundary.
4. ALPHA had responsibility for the jewel before sealing and therefore opportunity before departure.
5. ALPHA describes a distinctive feature of the original with a precision inconsistent with his claimed limited handling.
6. GAMMA's procedural violation explains suspicious train-system behavior but not the jewel's pre-departure state.

The final solution should come from proving that the crime window itself was false.

## Dramatic spine

1. Spectacle: blackout, alarm, apparent impossible theft.
2. GAMMA looks immediately suspicious because he controls the train and is visibly evasive.
3. Evidence confirms he broke procedure, strengthening the apparent solution.
4. His secret is real but cannot explain an earlier visual discrepancy.
5. BETA helps the player reframe the problem from “How was the case opened?” to “When did the real jewel last certainly exist?”
6. A pre-departure record moves the crime before the journey.
7. ALPHA's precision and authority, previously reassuring, become evidence of privileged access and knowledge.
8. The player accuses the man whose entire ROLE was built around protecting the object.

## Character-value target

- ALPHA: the same clarity and control that made him a protector in Work A can make him a formidable planner here.
- BETA: the same human-centered instinct that became destructive in Work A becomes an investigative strength here.
- GAMMA: the same boundary-testing energy survives inside an official uniform rather than requiring an outlaw ROLE.

## Aftertaste

The tester should feel that the world and biographies were radically different while the three PRINCES remained recognizably themselves.

The strongest desired spontaneous reaction is:

> “I want to see what role each of them gets next.”

## Prototype constraint

No explanation is given for why these PRINCES appeared in another era or work. They are recurring stars, not continuous fictional biographies.

# Authoring -> Compiler -> Runtime Model

Status: WORKING

## Principle

Do not make AI the product architecture. Treat AI models as replaceable creation capabilities.

The durable pipeline is:

1. Whole Work Design
2. Experience Semantic Model
3. Validation
4. Realization Compilation
5. Runtime Playback

## 1. Whole Work Design

Creation begins with the work as a complete entertainment experience, not with game widgets or reusable mechanics.

A MASTER STORY / MASTER WORK document should capture the whole concept before decomposition: hook, cast drama, full truth, central mystery, major reversals, climax, ending, and intended aftertaste.

AI may assist ideation, critique, consistency checking, rewriting, and expansion, but the whole-work concept remains the source of creative coherence.

## 2. Experience Semantic Model

The approved work is normalized into the EXPERIENCE CONTRACT. This step translates artistic intent into structured semantic information without committing to a UI.

## 3. Validation

Validation should operate at multiple levels:

- story coherence
- mystery solvability and fairness
- contradiction/causality consistency
- character consistency against PRINCE CORE
- dramatic effectiveness
- capability feasibility
- asset feasibility

AI can assist, but human editorial judgment remains the release authority in the initial phase.

## 4. Realization Compilation

A realization compiler/adapter maps the EXPERIENCE CONTRACT into the concrete content package expected by Runtime v1.

The initial implementation may be simple and partially manual. The architectural goal is that new runtime technologies can later introduce new compilers without redefining the EXPERIENCE.

## 5. Runtime Playback

Runtime executes a REALIZATION. Runtime may know about screens, buttons, animations, device input, 2D/3D, voice, and other implementation details. EXPERIENCE does not.

## AI role in the initial product

AI is primarily used at authoring time. Runtime gameplay should not require per-session LLM API calls in the initial release.

The reason is product quality, economics, latency, predictability, and operational simplicity—not an assumption that runtime AI will never be useful.

## Long-term view

As AI improves, more creation stages may become automated, and future runtimes may use AI during play. Those changes should replace or extend capabilities around the stable EXPERIENCE layer rather than redefine the platform core.

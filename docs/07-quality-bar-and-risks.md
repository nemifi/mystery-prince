# 07 — Quality Bar and Risks

## Primary risk: AI slop

The central product risk is not simply logical inconsistency. It is releasing large amounts of content that feels generic, shallow or mechanically assembled.

If that happens, the “AI-generated” positioning does not save the brand. Users may tolerate variability, but the IP still accumulates a quality reputation.

## Quality bar

A publishable mystery should satisfy multiple dimensions.

### Work quality
- strong hook
- one clear core appeal
- coherent pacing
- satisfying reversal/climax
- appropriate ending/aftertaste

### Mystery quality
- truth is internally coherent
- key inference is fair
- required information is obtainable
- no arbitrary late fact is required for resolution
- culprit/method logic withstands scrutiny
- misdirection is meaningful rather than random noise

### Character quality
- Princes are recognizable and appealing
- role does not erase PRINCE CORE
- the mystery creates interesting emotional pressure on the characters
- no need to protect popular Princes from difficult roles by default

### Game quality
- the user makes meaningful deductions/decisions
- play is not merely reading generated prose
- interaction fits mobile use
- session scope is appropriate for ~30 minutes

### Production quality
- assets are visually coherent
- voices/text/expressions match
- no broken references or impossible states
- content passes editorial/safety/legal review appropriate to release

## AI disclosure and responsibility

The project may openly frame works as AI-assisted/generated during production, and this can appropriately set expectations about the generative nature of the catalog.

However, disclosure should **not** be treated as a blanket liability or responsibility shield. Published content remains a product decision and should be reviewed accordingly.

## Other major risks

- **Canon creep:** accidentally rebuilding a restrictive shared universe.
- **UI lock-in:** encoding mysteries around today’s interaction method.
- **Over-formula:** every 30-minute mystery begins to feel identical.
- **Asset repetition:** reuse becomes visibly cheap rather than elegantly modular.
- **Character dilution:** recurring Princes lose identity when roles change too freely.
- **Mystery dilution:** character marketing makes culprit choices predictable or safe.
- **Production bottleneck relocation:** AI reduces writing cost but human QA becomes the new unscalable bottleneck.
- **Voice/content cost:** character-first premium presentation can still be expensive even if story generation is cheap.

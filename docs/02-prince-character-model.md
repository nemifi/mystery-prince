# 02 — Prince / Character Model

## Core model

### DECIDED
**The character continues; the biography does not have to.**

A recurring Prince should be understood similarly to a star who can play different roles in different works.

Example:
- one work: REI is a surgeon and suspect
- another work: REI is a detective
- another work: REI is the culprit
- another work: REI is a nobleman in a historical setting

These works do not need to share continuity.

## PRINCE CORE

### DECIDED
Every Prince will eventually have a **PRINCE CORE**: the elements that make the character recognizable across roles.

We are intentionally *not* fully specifying the schema yet. The important decision is the existence of this separation.

### Stable layer (examples, not final schema)
- visual identity
- voice identity
- characteristic speech rhythm
- emotional tendencies
- decision-making tendencies
- interpersonal distance
- recurring behavioral charm
- the fundamental reason users find the character appealing

### Variable layer (ROLE)
May change per work:
- profession
- detailed age
- era
- social status
- family
- personal history
- relationships with other Princes
- relationship with player/protagonist
- suspect / culprit / investigator / accomplice / victim-related role
- moral position
- fate

## Why this matters

A conventional long-running character timeline creates restrictions:
- popular characters become hard to make culprits
- death becomes difficult
- romance continuity conflicts across users/content
- profession and setting become locked
- AI story generation must carry enormous canon state

The star/role model keeps the IP asset while restoring narrative freedom.

## Character-first does not mean mystery-second

The business/IP may be character-first, but a mystery work must be structurally credible and enjoyable.

The ideal relationship is:

> Character emotion increases the stakes of deduction.

Examples:
- the user wants to trust a favorite character but evidence points at him
- a familiar personality behaves in an unusual way, creating meaningful suspicion
- a Prince’s recognizable personality changes how a role is performed, without determining guilt

This allows character attachment to amplify mystery rather than replace it.

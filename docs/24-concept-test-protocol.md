# H1/H2 Concept Test Protocol

Status: WORKING

## Purpose

Test the two highest-risk product hypotheses before designing a full commercial Runtime.

- H1: recurring PRINCES can change ROLE/world/biography and still accumulate character appeal.
- H2: character emotion and mystery reasoning strengthen each other.

## Test material

Use the same three prototype PRINCES in two independent polished vertical slices based on:

- `THE 23:30 MESSAGE`
- `THE SEALED EXPRESS`

Target approximately 10–15 minutes per slice for the first qualitative round.

The slices should not explain the star-system theory at length. Users should experience the recasting rather than being persuaded to accept it.

## Participant mix

Recruit from the intended broad audience but include different motivation profiles:

- character-game leaning
- mystery-game leaning
- users who already enjoy both

The first round is qualitative discovery, not market sizing.

## Order control

Counterbalance order:

- roughly half play A then B
- roughly half play B then A

This reduces the chance that one ROLE becomes unconsciously treated as the “original biography.”

## Before play

Collect only lightweight context:

- familiarity with character-focused mobile games
- familiarity with mystery/puzzle games
- what made them choose a character/content item from the prototype landing surface

Do not explain which character is meant to be suspicious or lovable.

## During play

Observe rather than over-question.

Capture moments where users naturally:

- name a favorite
- defend a character
- change suspicion because of affection
- distrust a character because of presentation rather than evidence
- voluntarily inspect a character-specific clue
- react strongly to a ROLE-specific reveal
- recognize a PRINCE behavior from the other work

The strongest H2 evidence is emotional conflict with reasoning, e.g. “I don't want it to be him, but...” rather than a high character-rating score alone.

## Between the two works

Ask lightweight questions without introducing the intended answer:

- Who stood out to you?
- What did you think was distinctive about each person?
- Who would you want to see again?

Do not ask “Did they feel like the same character?” until after the second work.

## After the second work

Core questions:

1. What felt the same about each recurring character?
2. What felt meaningfully different?
3. Did the changed ROLE feel fun, neutral, or confusing?
4. Did you ever make a mystery judgment because you liked or trusted a character?
5. Did the mystery change how attractive/interesting a character felt?
6. Which character would you most want to see in a third, completely different work?
7. What ROLE would you be excited to see that character play next?

## Strong positive signals

Look especially for spontaneous language such as:

- “this version of him”
- “he's still him even though...”
- “this time he's...”
- “I trusted him because...”
- “I didn't want to suspect him”
- “what role will he have next?”

These phrases imply the user has separated durable star identity from temporary ROLE without needing system terminology.

## Strong negative signals

- users assume the works share continuity and become confused
- users treat the second ROLE as a completely unrelated character despite shared presentation
- ROLE change destroys rather than expands attraction
- mystery can be skipped without reducing character impact
- characters can be removed without reducing mystery impact
- users repeatedly ask which biography is the “real” one

## Decision gate

Do not turn early qualitative results into false precision.

After the first round, make one of three calls:

- **CONTINUE** — H1/H2 signals are strong enough to invest in product Runtime design.
- **ITERATE** — the idea shows value but PRINCE CORE, casting contrast, or mystery integration needs another prototype round.
- **RETHINK** — users fundamentally require persistent biography or character/mystery layers do not reinforce each other.

Only after the format survives this gate should retention systems, monetization architecture, or large-scale AI content production become primary design concerns.

# Prototype PRINCE Performance Bible

Status: WORKING / CONCEPT TEST

Purpose: keep REI / MINATO / KAI recognizably the same PRINCE across different ROLEs without turning their CORE into a fixed biography.

This document controls **performance**, not lore.

## REI

### Audience promise

When a situation becomes ambiguous, REI makes it feel structured. The attraction is not that he always knows the answer; it is that he can decide what matters next.

### Speech rhythm

- short declarative sentences
- low use of filler or emotional softening
- asks precise questions rather than broad questions
- pauses before irreversible statements
- rarely repeats himself

### Emotional expression

Normal: still, economical, observant.

Under pressure: becomes more precise rather than louder.

True destabilizer: someone takes a risk for him or trusts him beyond what he believes he deserves.

### Character-specific mystery pleasure

The player should enjoy asking:

> Is he calm because he is competent, or because he already knows the truth?

### Do not drift into

- generic emotionless genius
- permanently moral authority
- detective by default
- cruel dominance for its own sake
- exposition machine

### Slice A performance

ROLE: hotel security director.

He controls the scene and initially looks suspicious because he controls records. When exposed, he does not beg for trust; he admits exactly what he falsified and why.

Anchor line:

> 「記録は俺が変えた。そこは否定しない。だが、その嘘と死因を同じものにするな。」

### Slice B performance

ROLE: exhibition curator / diplomatic aide; culprit.

His control becomes incriminating. Even as culprit, his behavior should still feel like REI: he created a clean false crime window because he hates uncontrolled ambiguity.

Anchor line before accusation:

> 「封印は破られていない。なら、列車内で起きたという前提から捨てるべきだ。」

The irony is that his own decision principle helps the player find him.

## MINATO

### Audience promise

MINATO notices the person before the problem. He creates trust through attention rather than theatrical sweetness.

### Speech rhythm

- uses the other person's name or direct reference naturally
- slightly longer sentences than REI
- gives emotional room; silence is part of the performance
- gentle humor under tension
- does not constantly praise or flatter

### Emotional expression

Normal: warm, grounded, attentive.

Under pressure: warmth narrows into intense focus.

True destabilizer: someone exploits trust, vulnerability, or a person he has chosen to protect.

### Character-specific mystery pleasure

The player should enjoy asking:

> Do I trust him because the facts support him, or because I want to?

### Do not drift into

- generic perfect boyfriend
- passive healer
- innocence by default
- constant romantic language
- emotional manipulation in every scene

### Slice A performance

ROLE: victim's physician; culprit.

His warmth must be real. He is not pretending to care about the player. That is what makes accusing him emotionally difficult.

Anchor line early:

> 「急がなくていい。君が見たものから、一つずつでいいよ。」

Anchor line after correct accusation:

> 「……そっか。君は、俺を信じたかったんだね。それでも見つけた。」

### Slice B performance

ROLE: railway investigator; genuine ally.

The same intimacy that was dangerous in Slice A is useful here. He notices testimony and emotional context that procedural investigators ignore.

Anchor line:

> 「『どう盗んだか』を急がなくていい。最後に本物だったのは、いつ？」

## KAI

### Audience promise

KAI makes certainty unstable. His attraction is the feeling that he may help, mislead, or test the player, but will rarely be boring.

### Speech rhythm

- verbal reversals
- answers with sharper questions
- partial truths that remain technically honest
- playful timing under danger
- occasionally names the assumption everyone else is unconsciously making

### Emotional expression

Normal: amusement, curiosity, light provocation.

Under pressure: becomes faster and more playful until genuinely cornered.

True destabilizer: loss of agency; being owned, trapped, or defined by someone else.

### Character-specific mystery pleasure

The player should enjoy asking:

> Is he hiding the truth, or showing me that I am asking the wrong question?

### Do not drift into

- random chaos
- permanent criminal role
- compulsory flirting
- omniscient trickster
- jokes that destroy mystery tension

### Slice A performance

ROLE: invited illusionist; red herring.

He possesses illicit access but treats suspicion almost as a game. His real function is to make the player question the theatricality of the 23:30 message.

Anchor line:

> 「いちばん綺麗な答えほど、誰かが見せたがってる答えだったりするよ？」

### Slice B performance

ROLE: chief conductor; institutional red herring.

The fun is inversion: KAI now wears the uniform of order. He still bends one rule, but for a reason unrelated to the jewel.

Anchor line:

> 「この列車じゃ、俺が規則なんだよ。……だから、どこを破ったかも俺が一番よく知ってる。」

## Cross-ROLE test standard

After both slices, a tester should be able to describe the three without mentioning profession:

- REI: the man who reduces ambiguity and takes control of the next decision
- MINATO: the man who closes human distance and makes trust feel natural
- KAI: the man who destabilizes certainty and protects his freedom

If testers mainly describe them as “security guy / doctor / conductor,” the CORE is not carrying enough of the identity.
